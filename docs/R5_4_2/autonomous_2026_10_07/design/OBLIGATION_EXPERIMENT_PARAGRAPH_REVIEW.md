# Invariants, obligations and experiment model mapping

Complete Data sections 31, 34, 39, 41, 43 and 45 govern this static mapping. Exact runtime chronology, observation capture and substantive judgments remain required independently of schema validity.

Data 31 paragraphs 0–3: immutable invariant revisions retain source, statement, scope, category/origin, activation policy and input cut. No materiality backlink is added. The invariant is accepted before its assessment; an obligation selects the exact applicable assessment. Activation policy must equal prior accepted/pinned context and cannot activate itself in the creating commit. Paragraphs 4–6 retain ACTIVE/SUPERSEDED/RETIRED/INVALIDATED and prohibit global evidence-depth authority; concrete requirements belong to obligations. Paragraph 7 is a separator.

Data 34 paragraphs 0–4: the coverage ledger is a projection. Stable key identity comprises exact source, target, invariant logical identity, scenario, environment and policy key. The successor wire key uses its declared /2 digest domain; historical /1 bytes remain unchanged. Changed target tuple creates a new key and explicit backward lineage; changed predicate/qualification at the same key creates a new obligation revision. Logical obligation IDs remain one-to-one handles in their campaign namespace and cannot change denominator identity.

Paragraphs 5–8: obligation revisions retain all exact source/target/invariant, environment, materiality, technique, independence, acceptance, falsifier/control, applicability, policy and origin requirements. Permitted earlier content can supply target/invariant facts, but cannot supply new governing policy/profile semantics. The prior accepted materiality assessment must apply to the exact invariant or target and be MATERIAL unless the exact pinned policy expressly admits another class. The assessment is not copied into a scalar or inferred from the obligation label.

Paragraphs 9–14: qualification status is UNASSESSED/IN_PROGRESS/QUALIFIED/BLOCKED/STALE; substantive outcome is separate and present only when semantically available. A qualified investigation may confirm a defect, so VIOLATION_CONFIRMED is not an execution failure. INCONCLUSIVE cannot satisfy a decisive-outcome requirement. Unresolved contradictions remain explicit blocking dependencies.

Paragraphs 15–21: applicability is an exact accepted obligation-specific decision with policy/scope/evidence/cut and APPLICABLE/NOT_APPLICABLE/UNKNOWN/CONFLICTED. A generic approval cannot substitute for it. Waiver is the distinct authorized COVERAGE_OBLIGATION_WAIVER decision with exact obligation in related refs. APPROVED waiver does not fulfill the original obligation or turn it into N/A. Predicate changes require new revisions; target changes require a new key/lineage. Paragraphs 22–23 retain the investigation-versus-outcome distinction and revision rule; the separator creates no extra obligation.

Data 39 paragraphs 0–5: hypotheses remain immutable source/scope/invariant/obligation-bound revisions, distinct from findings. Optional discovery origin is a prior reference. PROPOSED/PREREGISTERED/TESTING/CONFIRMED/REJECTED/UNRESOLVED/BLOCKED remain distinct; rejected hypotheses are retained and changed statement/scope creates a new revision. Paragraph 6 is a separator.

Data 41 paragraphs 0–3: ExperimentSpec retains hypothesis, invariant, obligations, actual source/variant/environment/dependencies/harness/fixtures, trigger, safe/buggy expectations, observation requirements, falsification, controls, optional fault, seed, cleanup, planning mode and optional preregistration cut. Material execution requires the specified prior immutable intent, with the explicitly declared exploratory exception; exploratory work cannot be relabelled preregistered after observing its result. Fuzz/property preregistration covers generator/oracle/bounds rather than every generated input. Descriptor and capture chronology must also satisfy Data 43 and the selected method/evidence policy. Paragraph 4 is a separator.

Data 43 paragraphs 0–4: ExecutionDescriptor separates intent from later result and retains exact prior experiment/source/environment plus qualified variant/dependencies/harness/fixtures/runner and input cut. Runtime records point back to that exact descriptor. ExecutionResult selects start/finish observations, real exit, optional activation, raw observations, controls, cleanup, optional reset and status. Original timestamps are informational; no observation/activation/cleanup record points to future ExecutionResult. Result use requires accepted observation/evidence qualification. Mechanical content-or-prior descriptor permission cannot bypass the prior execution/capture predicate. Paragraphs 5–7 retain EXECUTED/INCOMPLETE/BLOCKED/HARNESS_FAILURE/RESOURCE_BLOCKED and a separator.

Data 45 paragraphs 0–5: the independently raw-backed FaultRun/2 wrapper retains the complete declared fault run, exact prior fault/descriptor, activation observations, scope and cleanup. Descriptor cannot be an ExecutionResult. Fault record precedes its result dependency, and cleanup/activation observations cannot gain future result backlinks. ACTIVATED/NOT_ACTIVATED/PARTIALLY_ACTIVATED/BLOCKED/HARNESS_FAILURE retain their actual outcomes. Original complete payload equality, capture provenance and code dependencies are required; caller activation labels alone establish no activation. Paragraph 6 is a separator.

Remaining proof: exact key/ID namespace and lineage uniqueness; source/subject/materiality equality and cut visibility; full required obligation/evidence/control enumeration; finite policy-context selection; qualified applicability and independent waiver authority; preregistration/exploration and real execution intent; full observation/fault/cleanup capture; downstream staleness and no omission; retry/concurrency, durable rejection and migration. These static mappings do not qualify execution, substantive outcomes or completion.
